Forever drops the realm-selection screen. You choose the kind of world you want first, and each ruleset works as one broad community instead of a single server. The goal is to make it easy to find people who play the way you do while the world still feels busy and lived in.

## The rulesets

| Ruleset | Who it is for | Factions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | Questing, exploring and cooperating, with PvP when you opt in | Both factions on one account |
| PvP | Every trip into contested territory can turn into a fight | One faction only |
| Roleplaying | Players who want to stay in character, with strict naming and behavior rules | Both factions |
| Roleplay-PvP | Roleplaying’s naming and behavior rules in a world where you are almost always at risk of attack | Not announced yet |
| Hardcore | One life; death carries lasting consequences. Not in the game yet, and Blizzard has given no date | Both factions |

Roleplay-PvP was not part of the original plan: Blizzard added it on October 22 after player feedback. That made four rulesets at launch on November 4, with Hardcore to follow.

## Names

Each character has two names, and together they must be unique across your whole region. Names do not belong to a ruleset, so a name taken on one ruleset is taken on all of them.

## Playing together

- You can group, run dungeons and raid only with players in the **same ruleset**. Cross-ruleset groups are not supported.
- **Battlegrounds** can include players from other rulesets, except Hardcore, which stays separate.
- **Factions stay apart**: Horde and Alliance cannot group, on any ruleset.
- **Normal and PvP stay apart at launch**, mainly so each ruleset keeps its own economy and farming. If the Roleplay rulesets ever run low on players, Blizzard has mentioned sharing auction houses or dungeons with them, but says nothing is planned.

## Layering at launch

Each ruleset is one world, split into copies, or layers, when it gets busy. Blizzard went through the launch setup on its podcast of October 29, [All About Tech](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/topic/2395799):

- **Joining a group or a guild no longer moves you on the spot.** You get the world refresh notice with a time window and move when you are ready. In the beta an invite could move a whole group at once; Blizzard called that a bug.
- **Forced refreshes are much rarer** than in the beta, and the warning still comes first.
- **A zone, or a small group of zones, shares a layer.** Blizzard can regroup zones and adjust population caps as crowds move, so crossing into another zone can still refresh the world around you.
- **The game tries to keep you near your people**: the players you group with, your guild, nearby guilds and players who use your client language.
- **Fewer players share a crowded zone** since Blizzard's hotfixes of November 9, after players said zones felt overcrowded, and fewer still on the PvP ruleset.
- **Ignoring someone does not remove them from your world.**

## Faction balance on PvP

On the PvP ruleset you can only create characters for one faction: once you have an Alliance character there, you cannot make a Horde one, and the reverse. Blizzard also plans to limit new character creation for a faction temporarily when one side outnumbers the other, as it did in Season of Discovery.

## Moving between rulesets

You cannot hop freely. To play under a different ruleset, create a new character there. Collections and Legacy Points are account-wide across rulesets, with Hardcore handled differently:

- Progress, collections and rewards earned in Hardcore can carry over to the other rulesets.
- Progress from the other rulesets does not flow back into Hardcore.
- When a Hardcore character dies, it can transfer to another ruleset, including PvP.

## Language preference

You can set a preferred online language so you meet players who share it. Open the Game Menu (Escape), then Options, then Languages and Social under System, and choose a Preferred Online Language. The setting is account-wide and has a 24-hour cooldown.

## Which one should you pick?

- Pick **Normal** if you want the classic leveling experience without being ganked, or you want to play both factions with friends.
- Pick **PvP** if world PvP is the point. Commit to a faction with your friends before your first character there.
- Pick **Roleplaying** if you value immersion and naming conventions.
- Pick **Roleplay-PvP** if you want to stay in character and still fight for your faction in the open world.
- Wait for **Hardcore** if you want stakes. Your Legacy challenges earned in Hardcore will count everywhere else too.
