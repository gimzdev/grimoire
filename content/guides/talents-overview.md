All twenty-seven talent trees were rebuilt for Forever. Of the 467 talents, 121 are new and 298 work differently from Classic. Another 85 Classic talents are gone, many of them because their effect became a trainer spell or merged into another talent. The rules have not changed: one point per level from 10 to 60, 51 in total, and each row opens after five points in that tree.

Sixteen of the twenty-eight capstones are new talents (Feral has two). Nine more changed: Bloodthirst now also gives movement speed, Shadowform halves Shadow spell costs and doubles their crit damage, Lightning Mastery (Elemental Mastery under a new name) speeds up Lightning Bolt and lifts Chain Lightning's cooldown, and Moonkin Form doubles Omen of Clarity procs. Only Mortal Strike, Bestial Wrath and Adrenaline Rush work as you remember them. The Paladin, Druid, Mage, Priest and Hunter trees changed again in the launch client's hotfix cache of October 30, and Blizzard's development notes of November 2 confirmed those changes and rebuilt the Rogue, Shaman and Warrior trees: Mutilate now caps Assassination, Death Mark replaces Thousand Cuts, and Call of Thunder takes Lava Burst's place. The server's hotfixes of November 3 put those trees in the game, with a few talents in other columns than the notes suggested, and took Raging Blows out of Fury. Since launch Blizzard's hotfixes have changed numbers and rules rather than trees, the largest on November 10 for Enhancement shamans and Affliction warlocks.

## Five themes across every class

**Weapon talents adapt to your weapon.** Warriors lost Axe, Mace, Polearm and Sword Specialization and gained [[talent:warrior:Weaponmaster]]. Rogues lost four weapon specializations and gained [[talent:rogue:Hack and Slash]]. One talent, whatever you wield, so a weapon upgrade never forces a respec.

**Stats convert.** Several talents turn a stat your armor already carries into one your spec needs. Hunters get Attack Power from Intellect through [[talent:hunter:Careful Aim]], Enhancement shamans get both Attack Power and spell power from it through [[talent:shaman:Mental Dexterity]] and [[talent:shaman:Mental Quickness]], and Retribution paladins get spell damage through [[talent:paladin:Champion of the Light]].

**Every caster can regenerate while casting.** Paladins have [[talent:paladin:Reverence]], shamans [[talent:shaman:Mindfulness]], druids [[talent:druid:Reflection]], priests [[talent:priest:Meditation]], mages [[talent:mage:Arcane Meditation]] and hunters [[talent:hunter:Bestial Discipline]].

**Debuffs became personal.** [[talent:priest:Shadow Weaving]], [[talent:mage:Improved Scorch]], [[talent:mage:Winter's Chill]] and [[talent:warlock:Improved Shadow Bolt]] now buff only your own damage. Raids no longer need to stack a class to share its debuff.

**Core spells moved to the trainer.** Blessing of Kings, Consecration, Aimed Shot, Omen of Clarity, Nature's Grasp, Tactical Mastery and Divine Spirit are all trainer spells now, so those trees spend the points on something else. Lightwell and Magic Attunement are gone.

## Hit talents moved around

Several classes got their hit from a new place, which changes which off-tree points are mandatory:

- Mages get theirs from [[talent:mage:Precision]] in Frost, which now covers every school; Arcane Focus is gone.
- Warlocks get 5% hit to every spell from [[talent:warlock:Suppression]].
- Elemental shamans get theirs from [[talent:shaman:Tidal Focus]] in Restoration.
- Balance druids have [[talent:druid:Nature's Reach]]; Shadow priests [[talent:priest:Shadow Focus]].
- Paladins have [[talent:paladin:Precision]], which covers attacks and spells alike, and [[talent:paladin:Divine Precision]] for Holy spells; Discipline priests have [[talent:priest:Holy Precision]].
- Rogues have [[talent:rogue:Precision]], Survival hunters [[talent:hunter:Surefooted]], and Fury warriors [[talent:warrior:Furious Precision]] for the off-hand.

How much hit each role needs, against raid bosses and players, is in the [stats guide](/guides/stats-and-combat).

## Secondary Talents and respecs

Blizzard opens Dual Specialization at level 40; the game calls it Secondary Talents. It gives you a second set of talents, so you can keep a leveling build and a dungeon, healing or raid build and switch between them. You change talents, Legacy talents included, at your class trainer in a major city. Icy Veins and players report 50 gold for the second set; Blizzard has not posted the price. Since November 13 your action bars no longer reset at logout once you have it.

A respec costs gold at your trainer, and players report 1 gold the first time, then 5, rising with each reset to 50 at most. Every Grimoire leveling build says how many points separate it at 60 from its PvE build, from none to 18, so you know what a respec buys.

## Using the calculator

The [talent calculator](/talents) is built for planning as well as theorycrafting:

- **Left-click** adds a point, **right-click** or **Shift-click** removes one. On a phone, tap a talent and use the plus and minus buttons.
- New talents carry a green marker and changed ones a gold marker. Hover any talent to see its current and next rank and, for changed talents, what it did in Classic.
- The **point order** strip under the trees records the order you spent points in. It doubles as a leveling plan and shows the level each point unlocks.
- Turn on **Talented** to model the Legacy perk that unlocks each talent point five levels early.
- **Copy link** saves the whole build, order included, in a URL you can share. When the trees move, a link from before still opens the talents it was made with.

Every class guide links its builds straight into the calculator.
